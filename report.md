Q1. The largest bidirectional human-label confusions were automobile/truck (2,373 images); cat/dog (1,758 images); deer/horse (916 images). Across the 45,000 training images, 18,109 labels were wrong (40.24%). The matrix uses clean classes as rows and human labels as columns. These concentrated transitions suggest structured mistakes, although class-level counts alone do not establish the cause of individual errors.

Q2. The diagnostic criterion first flagged epoch 35: wrong-label training accuracy rose 3.15 points while clean validation accuracy fell 3.99 points, comparing consecutive three-epoch means. This early warning does not establish a permanent transition. From the noisy-selected epoch 43 to epoch 60, wrong-label training accuracy rose from 22.85% to 69.12%, while clean validation accuracy fell from 78.38% to 69.26%. That later divergence provides stronger evidence of harmful memorization. Clean accuracy was diagnostic only; early stopping used noisy validation accuracy.

Q3. Out-of-fold negative log probability performed best in the post-hoc comparison (AUROC 0.9280). The submitted scores achieved precision@18109 of 81.31%. The score and checkpoint rules were fixed before clean-label evaluation. A difficult, correctly labeled image can receive a high score because the model assigns little probability to its class. Conversely, systematic annotation errors can receive low scores when the model learns the same confusion. Held-out scoring reduces the influence of memorizing an image during training.

Q4. ELR (single seed 2133) reached 58.06% noisy validation accuracy, +2.12 points over early-stopped cross-entropy. Its temporal prediction targets resist fitting incorrect annotations. At epoch 60, ELR retained 57.78% accuracy versus 50.18% for cross-entropy, consistent with reduced late memorization. Two seeds and their equal-probability ensemble were compared using noisy validation only. Architecture, optimizer, augmentation and schedule were shared, with 60 epochs per network. The extra seed increased training cost. These results support the mechanism but do not establish hidden-test performance.

Q5. The cross-entropy versus ELR ranking did not change: ELR improved noisy validation accuracy by +2.12 points with human labels and +3.38 with synthetic labels. Only these two methods were retrained on synthetic labels; this does not establish the ranking of all robust methods. Human errors concentrate in specific class pairs, whereas synthetic corruption is uniform. Despite matched overall noise rates, these different error structures can change the benefit of regularization. The configuration selected on human-noisy validation was retained; synthetic checkpoints used synthetic validation. The two noisy validation accuracies are not directly comparable measures of clean accuracy.
